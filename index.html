Safety and guidelines

This is not an emergency service. In an emergency call 000. For crisis support call Lifeline 13 11 14 (24 hours).

For everyone

  • Meet in a public place first, like a library, cafe or shopping centre. Tell a friend where you're going.
  • No money handling. Volunteers never hold cash, cards, PINs or bank details, and never pay for things on someone's behalf.
  • Only share contact details when you're ready. The board keeps them hidden until both of you agree.
  • Keep health details and other people's names off the board.
  • You can say no or stop at any time.

For volunteers

  • Volunteers here are not NDIS providers and are not paid. This is informal, community help.
  • To be listed you must provide a Working With Children Check and a National Police Check. In NSW a WWCC is required by law for child-related volunteering. First aid is a bonus.
  • Volunteers are labelled "Checks provided (not yet verified by Coffeebreak)". Coffeebreak doesn't check them yet, so be ready to show your originals.
  • Use your own car insurance if you drive someone, and stay within what you're comfortable doing.

For families and carers

  • You can always ask to see a volunteer's checks before they start. A good volunteer won't mind.
  • Sight the original police check certificate and photo ID.
  • WWCC status can be verified online with the Office of the Children's Guardian (OCG). Organisations use the OCG employer portal. Parents and carers engaging someone for their child can use the OCG verify form or the Service NSW app. You need their family name, date of birth and WWC number. How to verify
  • Only go ahead with someone whose status is "Cleared".

Checks: police check and WWCC

Every volunteer on Coffeebreak Volunteer Support must provide both checks below. They help keep everyone safe.

National Police Check

A National Police Check (a Nationally Coordinated Criminal History Check) shows any criminal history that can be released about you. It's a certificate with an issue date, and it's only current at the time it's issued.

How to get one in NSW

  • NSW Police Force: apply online. It's online only, so there's no counter service at police stations. NSW Police: National Police Check
  • Volunteer rate: a volunteer check through NSW Police costs much less than the standard fee, but you need a secure link from a registered organisation. NSW residents can get one through The Centre for Volunteering. The Centre for Volunteering: police check
  • Accredited providers: you can also apply through an ACIC accredited body (many work online, Australia-wide). ACIC: accredited bodies

Fees change, so check the current price on the official page. NSW Police fees and FAQs: NSW Police NPC FAQs.

NSW Working With Children Check (WWCC)

The WWCC is a requirement by law for anyone who works or volunteers in child-related work in NSW. It includes a national police check and a review of reportable workplace misconduct. If you're cleared it lasts 5 years, and holders are continuously monitored. There's no card. You get a WWC number by email.

It's free for volunteers. Choose "Volunteer" as your check purpose.

How to apply

  1. Fill in the online application form. You'll get an APP number by email.
  2. Within 28 days, take your APP number and 4 original identity documents to a Service NSW centre to verify your ID.
  3. Screening can take up to 4 weeks. When you're cleared, your WWC number is emailed to you.

Apply on Service NSW OCG: Working With Children Check

Verifying a volunteer's WWCC

Families can ask to see a volunteer's checks before they start. WWCC status is verified online through the Office of the Children's Guardian (OCG):

  • Organisations and employers register with the OCG and verify through the employer portal.
  • Parents and carers engaging someone for their child can verify using the OCG online form or the Service NSW app (QR code). You need the person's family name, date of birth and WWC number.
  • Only go ahead with a "Cleared" status. Don't use anyone who is barred or not found.

OCG: what parents need to know · Service NSW: check a WWCC status

Outside NSW

Other states and territories have their own checks, for example the Blue Card in Queensland, the Working with Children Check in Victoria, Western Australia and South Australia, Working with Vulnerable People registration in the ACT and Tasmania, and the Ochre Card in the Northern Territory. Apply through your own state or territory government. A National Police Check can come from your state police or an ACIC accredited body.

Coffeebreak Volunteer Support shows volunteers as "Checks provided (not yet verified by Coffeebreak)". Coffeebreak doesn't review the checks yet, so always sight the originals and verify the WWCC yourself.
